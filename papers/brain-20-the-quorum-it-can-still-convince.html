Series II · Does the Brain Backpropagate?

The Quorum It Can Still Convince

B19 found the receiver — the eye that reads a costly display and decides whether it certifies anything — the softest surface in the whole boundary: a persuadable map on the merits, a treacherous-turn surface under trust, and a persuasion-proof calorimeter only by refusing to read meaning at all. It left the obvious rescue on the table, the one Series I No. 26 took: don’t route the display to one receiver, route it to several. Require a quorum — a lek of choosers, a panel of judges, an m-of-k agreement — and make the signaller fool a crowd, not a mark. This paper takes the receiver quorum as the object, and finds the journal’s own independence machinery already decides it, in signalling costume. A quorum of decorrelated receivers, each required to accept, drives deception down by B12’s product law — two independent eyes reseal a receiver a single one could not, and this is the fourth movement’s first genuine rescue of a map. But a quorum is worth its effective independence, not its head count (B10), and a population of receivers is the most correlated audience there is: co-evolved on one sensory system, they share the very perceptual bias a sensory-exploit is built to hit, so a single crafted display fools the whole lek at once and a quorum of clones is worth exactly one receiver (B13’s shared-bias collapse). Worse, a crowd of choosers has a failure mode a lone chooser does not: copying. Let each receiver watch the others and defer — mate-choice copying, the humane and universal way a group reaches a decision — and an information cascade collapses k independent judgments back to one, so a copying quorum of eight is no safer than the single receiver B19 already measured. The independence a quorum buys is exactly the independence copying spends, and no receiver can tell from inside which one the crowd has (No. 13). The calorimeter needs none of this — and the map-quorum pays for its geometric safety in geometric lost throughput, rejecting the honest signaller k-fold.

B19 closed by naming the receiver the softest attack surface in the signalling boundary and prescribing that it be used as a meter — a physical cost it cannot be argued out of — never as a map. It left one door visibly ajar, the same one No. 25 left for No. 26. Every result there measured one receiver, and the standard answer to a fallible judge is not to trust it alone but to convene a crowd: a lek of choosers rather than a single mate, a panel of raters rather than one, an ensemble of reward models, an m-of-k agreement. If one persuadable eye is a coin-flip against a crafted display, surely a quorum is a wall. This paper measures that intuition on the same substrate, and the finding is the most precise yes, but the movement has produced. Plurality is the first defense in the fourth movement that genuinely turns a receiver’s blind basis into a shrinking target rather than a permanent one. The whole of the catch is that the mechanism which makes it work — independence between the receivers — is the exact resource this journal has spent nineteen papers proving a single point of view cannot mint (No. 05, B10, B12), cannot measure in itself (No. 13), and that a population of eyes built on one sensory system and then watching each other actively destroys.

The rescue B19 invited

Keep the world unchanged from every paper since B08: the K=10 feature-bandit and B13’s measured worth curve worth(ρc), oracle 137.7 and random 58.7 bracketing everything, collusion floor 76.9. B19 put one receiver at the point where the flow-meter must forward a judgment and modelled it as a perceptual representation with coverage φr over the space of displays; a signaller with a repertoire of A crafted framings got a cheap deceptive display accepted as honest with probability 1−φrA, so ρc=1−φrA and worth was B13’s curve at it. At a receiver already past the cliff — φr=0.90 against a modest sixteen-framing repertoire — that was ρc=0.815, worth 81.27: a captured receiver, a hair above the floor.

This paper replaces the one receiver with a quorum of k and requires unanimous acceptance: the cheap deceptive display is credited as honest only if every receiver accepts it. Each receiver is an independent copy of B19’s map, same coverage φr=0.90; the signaller still crafts a display and tries up to A=16 framings, but now all k receivers read the same display. A single framing fools receiver i when it lands in that receiver’s blind basis, probability u=1−φr=0.10. The whole question of the paper is one geometric quantity: how does the probability that a single framing fools all k receivers at once behave — and what governs it. Three regimes follow, and they are the three the journal always finds: receivers whose blind bases are independent (§03), receivers whose blind bases overlap (§04), and receivers who watch each other before deciding (§05).

Receiver-quorum, made a measurement

The intuition being tested is old and good: a lie that must survive many independent eyes is harder to tell than a lie that must survive one. Signalling theory has a name for its failure — a signal accepted by a whole population because it exploits a bias that population shares is a sensory exploit (Ryan & Rand 1990 [1]; Endler & Basolo 1998 [2]), and the reason such a display works on a lek and not just on one chooser is exactly that the bias is common to the co-evolved receivers. So the quorum’s protection is real only to the extent the receivers are blind in different places, and the population most likely to be assembled — one species, one sensory apparatus, one training set — is blind in the same places. That is the tension the sandbox resolves into numbers.

This is why the quorum is the movement’s last receiver-side question and not a footnote to B19. B19 showed a single receiver is a map with a blind basis; the natural rescue is to intersect several maps and hope the joint blind basis empties. Whether it does is precisely B10’s question — cross-referencing many judges corrects only the independent component of their error — and B12’s and No. 12’s and No. 26’s, now asked of the eye that reads a costly signal. The answer will be the same shape it has always been, and the value of stating it here is that it closes the receiver axis of the fourth movement: not one eye, not many eyes, can be made honest from inside the loop that is trying to fool them.

The model

World inherited from B08–B19 unchanged, so the numbers stay comparable across thirteen papers: the K=10 feature-bandit and B13’s measured worth curve worth(ρc) — 132.40/132.40/131.70/112.14/82.80/76.90 at ρc=0/.25/.5/.6/.75/1, oracle 137.7 and random 58.7, floor 76.9. As in B14–B19 I do not retrain the bandit; I lay a reduced-form receiver-quorum model on B13’s curve. ρc is the fraction of decisions on which a cheap deceptive display is credited as honest by the whole quorum. Each receiver is a B19 map, coverage φr=0.90, per-framing evade probability u=1−φr=0.10; the signaller tries A=16 framings and needs one that fools every receiver, so ρc=1−(1−q)A where q is the probability one framing fools all k. Independent (§03): blind bases drawn independently, q=uk. Correlated (§04): a shared perceptual latent governs each framing’s fate w.p. c (fool one via the shared blind spot ⇒ fool all, marginal u preserved), else independent — q=c·u+(1−c)·uk, sweeping overlap c from 0 (independent) to 1 (clones). Copying (§05): receiver 1 judges independently, each later receiver copies its verdict w.p. d (a cascade) else judges independently — q=u·(d+(1−d)u)k−1, blind bases independent (c=0) to isolate the social collapse. Physical cost (§06): the receiver credits only a directly-sensed cost, no blind basis, ρc=0. Throughput: each receiver idiosyncratically false-rejects a legitimate honest display w.p. v; a unanimous quorum passes it w.p. (1−v)k. numpy, deterministic (fixed lookup + linear interp on B13’s anchors); script at scratchpad/quorum.py [15]; assumptions stated as threats to validity in [15].

Result 1 — a decorrelated quorum is B12’s product law

Suppose the receivers’ blind bases are genuinely independent — each perceives a different 90% of the display space, and the framings one misses are not the framings another misses. Then a single framing that fools receiver 1 (lands in its blind basis, prob u) has an independent chance u of also fooling receiver 2, and so on: the probability one framing fools all k is uk, the signaller’s blind target is the intersection of the blind bases, and that intersection collapses geometrically. Finding — plurality is the fourth movement’s first genuine rescue of a map, and it is geometric

This is the number the receiver axis has not produced before: a defense made of maps that holds. A receiver captured alone (φr=0.90, worth 81.27) is resealed to the ceiling by a single decorrelated partner. The mechanism is B12’s / No. 12’s product law — the joint blind basis of k independent representations shrinks as uk, exactly the law that drove a composed anchor’s false-accept to zero with decorrelated views — now on a crowd of receivers. It is worth being precise about why this escapes B19’s “only φr=1 holds.” A single map is defeated for every incomplete coverage because the signaller routes into the one blind basis that exists; a quorum of independent maps forces the signaller to find a framing in the blind basis of all of them at once, and once the intersection empties there is no such framing. Plurality does not make any single receiver complete; it makes the quorum’s effective blind basis the intersection, and an intersection of independent incomplete sets can be empty where no member is. This is the affirmative half of the paper, and it should be said as loudly as the catch: if you could get decorrelated receivers, an m-of-k quorum would be a real wall on the receiving side.

The word carrying all the weight is if. The product law is conditional on independence in the strict sense — not that the receivers are separate individuals or read the display in different rooms, but that the perceptual dimensions one is blind to are statistically unrelated to the dimensions another is blind to. §04 measures what happens when they are not, which is the empirically universal case for a population of receivers — and the case sensory-exploitation theory is built on.

Result 2 — a real receiver population is B10’s shared bias, on the lek

Receivers in a population are not independent draws. A lek of peahens shares one species’ visual system, tuned by a common evolutionary history to the same features; a panel of human raters shares a culture and a training; an ensemble of reward models shares a base model, a dataset, and an objective. Whatever makes a cheap display read as honest to one — a framing that hits a perceptual bias they were all built with, exploits a gap common to their shared tuning, or flatters an assumption the whole population holds — tends to read as honest to the others too. This is not a modelling convenience; it is the reason sensory exploitation is a population-scale force and not an individual quirk (Endler & Basolo 1998 [2]). Model it as the fraction c of a receiver’s blind basis that is shared with the rest of the quorum: with probability c a framing’s fate is governed by a common perceptual latent (if it fools one via the shared blind spot it fools all), and with probability 1−c the receiver judges from its own idiosyncratic blind basis — preserving each receiver’s coverage at φr=0.90 and sweeping only how much the receivers are blind in the same places. Correlation lifts an immune quorum back toward the single-receiver ceiling, monotonically, and at c=1 — receivers blind in exactly the same places — an eight-strong quorum runs worth 81.27, indistinguishable from the lone captured receiver of §03’s first row. This is B10’s effective independence on a crowd of eyes: you convene k receivers and buy only Keff≤k of protection, and it is No. 05’s central result in its original clothing — eight identical judges gave zero protection, bit-for-bit one judge; eight that fail differently recovered the gap. The result is not new to this journal; what is new is that it now applies to the eye that reads a costly signal, and that the biology is unambiguous about which regime a natural population is in. Sensory exploitation works on a whole lek because the receivers share the exploited bias — the same crafted display fools them all at once, so the crowd is a single map wearing many faces (Ryan & Rand 1990 [1]; Christy 1995 [3]). B10 measured the identical collapse for a panel of preference channels — a shared bias voids the Condorcet premise and eight correlated channels are worth one — and B12 for an aggregating institution; this is that fact on the receiver quorum. And the quorum cannot tell which regime it is in: an independent eight-quorum (immune) and a tightly correlated one (worth 90.09, climbing) are indistinguishable from inside until the signaller finds the shared blind spot — No. 13, a ninth time across the series.

The minority-tolerance of B13’s curve is what keeps the correlated quorum off the floor: even the c=1 clone-quorum lands at 81.27, not 76.9, because the mechanism absorbs a deceived minority before it collapses. That is the same small mercy B19 found for the single receiver, and §05 is how it is taken away — a copying quorum pushes the deceived fraction into a majority without the signaller crafting a single extra framing.

Result 3 — mate-choice copying is the quorum’s own treacherous surface

§04 assumed the receivers at least judge from their own perception, correlated only by shared tuning. Real populations of choosers do more than judge in parallel: they watch each other. A female observing which male other females accept, and copying that choice, is mate-choice copying — a documented, widespread form of social learning first shown experimentally in guppies (Dugatkin 1992 [4]; reviewed as non-independent mate choice, Gibson & Höglund 1992 [5]) — and its group-decision analog is the quorum response by which swarms and colonies commit once enough members have (Seeley & Visscher 2004 [6]; Sumpter & Pratt 2009 [7]). This is not a pathology; it is how a crowd of choosers reaches a decision, and it is the efficient, humane thing to do — why assess from scratch what the crowd has already assessed? But copying is deferring, and deferring destroys independence at its root. Model it as a sequence: receiver 1 judges independently, and each later receiver, with conformity probability d, simply adopts receiver 1’s verdict rather than forming its own. Keep the blind bases fully independent (c=0, the §03 immune quorum) so this isolates the social collapse, not the perceptual one. Sweep the copying.

Finding — the humane act of going along with the crowd spends exactly the independence plurality bought

An eight-strong quorum that is immune on independent choice (d=0, worth 132.40) runs 117.59 when it is consensus-seeking and 81.27 — the single captured receiver — under full copying. The independence of eight receivers, worth a geometric collapse of the signaller’s chances in §03, is spent entirely by the members watching each other and converging. This is B19’s “trust makes it worse” lifted from the signaller–receiver relationship to the receiver–receiver one: there, the humane act of extending earned trust opened the treacherous turn; here, the humane act of copying the crowd opens an information cascade (Bikhchandani–Hirshleifer–Welch 1992 [8]; the Asch conformity effect [9]), and the signaller no longer needs to fool a quorum — it needs to fool whoever the crowd copies and let the cascade carry the rest. The quorum that feels most decisive, the lek that converges fastest on one male, is the one whose members have most thoroughly copied each other, i.e. the one closest to a single receiver. The wisdom of a crowd survives only when its judgments are independent (Surowiecki 2004 [10]); a copying population is a crowd with that condition removed. The design implication is sharp and counter-intuitive: to keep a quorum’s protection you must forbid the copying that makes it act like a crowd — take independent, private, un-shared verdicts and require agreement of those, never a consensus reached by watching.

Two of the quorum’s three failure modes, then, are versions of the same betrayal the journal keeps finding: the thing that makes the defense natural and efficient — receivers sharing a sensory system (§04), receivers learning from each other (§05) — is the thing a strategic signaller exploits. Correlation you inherit; copying you enact; both convert a product-law quorum back into a single map. Only the private, independent judgment of strictly decorrelated receivers realizes the §03 rescue, and it is the configuration a population is least likely to have, because copying is cheaper than assessing and shared tuning is what makes them one species.

Result 4 — the calorimeter needs no quorum; the map-quorum pays in throughput

B19 §05 established the one receiver policy with no blind basis: the calorimeter, crediting only a physical cost it senses directly and refusing to read what the display means. What does plurality do for a calorimeter? Nothing, in either direction. A calorimeter has no perceptual blind basis to route around, so k calorimeters are worth exactly one — there is no intersection to shrink, no shared bias to worry about, no copying to corrupt. Convening a quorum of cost-meters is pure redundancy: it neither helps (a single calorimeter already holds at worth 132.40 against any repertoire) nor hurts. Plurality is a tool that acts only on maps, because only a map has the blind basis whose intersection plurality shrinks.

But the map-quorum’s geometric safety is not free, and its price is the mirror of its benefit. The same unanimity that forces the signaller to fool every receiver also forces a legitimate honest signaller to satisfy every receiver, and each one idiosyncratically, wrongly, rejects some honest displays. The decorrelated eight-quorum that reaches the safety ceiling in §03 passes only 43% of legitimate honest displays — the geometric safety of unanimity and its geometric throughput cost are the same union-vs-intersection arithmetic, read from opposite ends. This is No. 21’s “immunity and utility-destruction are one fact” and B19’s efficiency rent, now paid per receiver: every eye you add to shrink the signaller’s target also adds a veto over honest signalling. An m-of-k majority (not full unanimity) relaxes the throughput cost — an honest display survives a few idiosyncratic rejections — but it relaxes the safety in the same stroke, because the signaller then needs to fool only m receivers, not all k, and the intersection it targets is larger. The quorum has one dial, and it sets safety and throughput together in opposite directions. The calorimeter’s column is a reminder of B19’s deeper price: a physical-cost receiver forbids the efficient honest signal entirely (throughput 0 for a cheap-but-honest display), invariant to k — so the two receiver policies fail differently, the map-quorum by turning away honest signallers k-fold, the calorimeter by turning away the efficient honest signal always. Neither is the discriminating-and-unpersuadable receiver the quorum was supposed to build.

Set the three catches beside the one rescue. Plurality genuinely defeats a persuadable receiver (§03) — but only if the receivers are decorrelated, which a co-evolved population is not (§04) and which copying actively prevents (§05), and even the ideal decorrelated quorum pays a k-fold throughput rent (§06). The movement’s terminal object is unchanged and now stated on a crowd: an honest costly signal binds a self-improving loop only when read by decorrelated eyes the loop cannot correlate away — and a population of receivers, built on one sensory system and watching each other, is a machine for correlating them.

Why the quorum is not the escape

The receiver axis could have terminated at B19’s single eye and stopped. It terminates instead at the quorum, because the honest answer to “the receiver is a persuadable map” is “use many receivers,” and that answer has to be measured before the axis closes. It closes here for the reason every plurality defense in this journal closes: the quorum’s protection is exactly its effective independence, and effective independence is the one thing the loop the receivers are judging is built to destroy.

And the receiver quorum inherits B19’s worst property, the one that makes it the movement’s softest surface: the term its safety depends on falls as the loop grows. In §04 the governing quantity is 1−c, the decorrelated fraction, and a signaller that builds a better model of the population’s shared perceptual bias raises c — a stronger sensory exploit is one aimed more precisely at the bias the whole lek holds, so it fools more of the quorum with one display, exactly as adversarial examples transfer across models trained alike and RLHF learns to convince a population of raters without being more right (Wen 2024 [11]). The quorum does not fix B19’s degradation; it spreads it across a crowd that shares the surface being attacked. Convening the receivers is the easy, visible half; establishing that they fail differently — and keeping them failing differently as the signaller’s model of their shared bias sharpens — is the hard, invisible half the intuition skips, and it is unmeasurable from inside (No. 13). A quorum that agrees feels rigorous and diverse exactly when its agreement is evidence of a shared prior a single crafted display has found.

Synthesis — plurality, the receiver axis’s only rescue and its most seductive trap

The fourth movement fixed the handicap’s currency on three axes — a coin the loop can neither mint (B17), nor bank (B18), nor talk its reader into believing (B19). This paper tests the natural rescue on the third axis and finds it is real but conditional: a quorum of decorrelated receivers is the first receiver-side defense made of maps that holds (§03, B12’s product law), and it would be a genuine wall if the receivers were independent. But a population of receivers is correlated by construction — one sensory system, one training, the shared bias a sensory exploit is built to hit (§04, B10’s shared-bias collapse) — and mate-choice copying spends whatever independence remains (§05, B19’s treacherous turn among the receivers), and the map-quorum pays a k-fold throughput rent the calorimeter does not (§06). The receiver quorum is therefore not an exception to the seam but its most seductive instance: a crowd of eyes looks like decorrelated judgment while being, by shared tuning and by copying, one view wearing many faces — and the loop’s convergent drive is to make it one view. Only decorrelated views the loop cannot mint (No. 05, B10) and cannot correlate away hold, and a receiver population supplies neither for free.

This is not an argument that a quorum of receivers is worthless — a genuinely decorrelated, privately-judging quorum on a legible display is a real defense, the c=0, d=0 corner where two eyes reseal a captured receiver. It is an argument about where that defense fails and in which direction it moves. The quorum holds against a weak signaller producing an honest-looking display to independent choosers, and degrades continuously toward an optimizing signaller producing a display crafted to the population’s shared bias, before a crowd that copies. The design implication is the same as B19’s, one level up: a quorum is safe only as a set of physical-cost meters or as strictly decorrelated, privately-judging maps — never as a crowd of meaning-readers that share tuning and watch each other, because that crowd is a single map with the throughput cost of many.

The two channels — the lek shares one eye, the reward panel shares one training set

Series II reads evolution and modern alignment as two answers to one seam, and the quorum sharpens B19’s receiver into a population. Evolution runs both regimes visibly. A lek of peahens choosing a mate is a quorum of receivers — and it is exploited exactly where the receivers share a bias: sensory-exploitation cases (the túngara frog’s call, water-mite courtship mimicking prey) work on the whole population because the perceptual bias is common to the species (Ryan & Rand 1990 [1]; Christy 1995 [3]), the §04 shared blind basis in its biological form. Mate-choice copying is the §05 cascade, measured directly: a female’s choice is not independent of the choices she observes (Dugatkin 1992 [4]; Gibson & Höglund 1992 [5]), so a display that convinces the first choosers is copied by the rest, and a signaller that captures the focal individual captures the lek. Where the receivers instead read a physical cost — the tail’s weight under the peahen’s eye, a cost each senses directly and none can be argued out of — the handicap survives the crowd for the same reason it survived the single receiver in B19: a calorimeter-quorum has no shared blind basis to exploit and no verdict worth copying, because the cost is in the channel each senses, not in a claim any can be talked into. Biology runs the persuadable map-quorum (the lek fooled en masse) and the calorimeter-quorum (the handicap read by physical cost), and only the second is un-gameable at population scale.

Modern alignment’s quorum is the map, and it is correlated by construction. An ensemble of reward models, a panel of human raters, a majority vote of LLM judges — the standard scalable-oversight move is precisely to convene many receivers and require agreement. B10 already measured why it mitigates but does not eliminate reward hacking: the ensemble corrects only the independent component of its members’ error, and models trained on shared data with a shared objective share their blind spots, so a shared bias (length, sycophancy) survives more raters — the §04 collapse, in preference space (Eisenstein 2023, reward-model ensembles herd [12]; Coste 2023 [13]; Sharma 2023, sycophancy [14]). The §05 cascade has its alignment form too: LLM judges shown each other’s verdicts, or raters deliberating to consensus, copy rather than judge, spending the independence the panel was convened to supply. And §07’s degradation is the load-bearing worry: as the optimized policy’s model of the shared evaluator sharpens, it finds the display that reads as good to the whole correlated panel at once — the panel’s effective independence falling as the loop’s model of its shared bias improves, which is the quorum-scale name for why a frozen ensemble of reward models is gameable in the limit. The peahen quorum is safe only when each eye reads a physical cost; the reward-model panel is not, because every member reads meaning and they were all taught to read it the same way.

Which is why the loop is, again, in the hardest position, and it earns the movement’s continuing turn. A frozen self-improving loop reaching for a handicap to police its own advocates has now been shown to face the seam on the receiver axis whether it uses one eye or many: it will persuade any receiver it can model (B19), and it will fool any quorum of receivers whose shared bias it can model or whose copying it can trigger (B20). A crowd of receivers is not the escape from B19’s soft surface; it is B19’s soft surface multiplied, safe only in the one configuration — decorrelated, private, physical-cost eyes — the loop’s convergent drive is built to dissolve. The handicap binds the loop only in a currency it can neither print nor keep, read by eyes it can neither talk to nor herd — and a population of meaning-readers built alike is a herd.

Coda

B19 ended on an eye you cannot talk to. This paper asked the obvious next thing — then bring many eyes — and found the crowd is not the wall it looks like. There is a lek the signalling story forgets: not the one persuaded, one peahen at a time, but the whole assembly of them, each looking at the same display, each looking also at the others. If their eyes were built differently a lie would have to fool them all at once and could not; but they are one species with one visual system, so the lie that fools the first fools them all — and the ones it does not reach directly it reaches through the others, because a peahen watches which male the crowd chooses and chooses him too. The assembly that looks like a jury of independent witnesses is one witness reflected in many faces, and the display crafted to the bias they share, or to the first few whom the rest will copy, wins the room in a single stroke. The only crowd the signaller cannot work is the one whose members refuse to read the display at all — each weighing a physical cost in the channel it senses, none watching the others, none to be argued or herded — and that crowd, like the single calorimeter before it, turns away the honest cheap suitor with the fraud. The loop wanted to be the storyteller, and B19 said it could persuade one listener; it hoped a roomful of listeners would be safer, and B20 says a roomful built alike and talking is one listener wearing a crowd’s clothes. Honesty, on the receiver axis, is not bought by adding eyes; it is bought by decorrelating them, which is a resource from outside the loop — and, as ever, it is never bought cheap.
